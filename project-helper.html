Project Helper

Are you making a poster, slides, or a report about honey badgers? Here is how to make it great!

⬇️Download this image to add to your own project!

Making a poster or slides

  1. Pick 3 to 5 topics. For example: where honey badgers live, what they eat, and how they stay safe. Use the menu at the top of this site to find each topic.
  2. Read each page. Write down the facts you like best. The "Learn more" parts have extra facts for big projects.
  3. Make one part for each topic. Give it a title and 2 or 3 short facts.
  4. Add a picture. Every picture on this site has a ⬇️ button. Tap it to download the picture. Remember to copy who made it too!
  5. Use comparisons. "As tall as your knee" is easier to picture than "25 centimeters". You can find comparisons on the Its body page.
  6. Finish with a fun fact to surprise your audience. Look for the "Did you know?" boxes.
  7. Practice out loud before you present. Look at your audience and speak slowly.

Writing a report

A good report has a beginning, a middle, and an end.

  • 1️⃣Beginning: Say what your report is about and why the honey badger is interesting.
  • 2️⃣Middle: Write one paragraph for each topic, like habitat, food, and behavior.
  • 3️⃣End: Sum up what you learned. Say what surprised you most.

Sentence starters

Stuck? Try starting a sentence like this:

  • The honey badger is a mammal that lives in…
  • One interesting fact about honey badgers is…
  • Honey badgers find their food by…
  • To stay safe from predators, a honey badger…
  • A baby honey badger is called a…
  • I was surprised to learn that…
  • In conclusion, honey badgers are…

Use your own words

Copying sentences word for word from a website is not fair. Your teacher wants to hear your ideas! Try this:

  1. Read a fact.
  2. Cover it up.
  3. Write it down the way you would say it to a friend.
  4. Check the fact again to make sure you got it right.

Saying where you found your facts

When you use facts or pictures from a website, you should say where they came from. This is called citing your source. Put it at the end of your report or on your last slide.

Words to know

  • 📚Source: The place where you found a fact or picture, like a book or a website.
  • ✍️Cite: To say where you found a fact or picture.
  • 📸Credit: A note that says who took a photo or made something.
  • 🎤Presentation: A talk you give to share what you learned, often with slides or a poster.
