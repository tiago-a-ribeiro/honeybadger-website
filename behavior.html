Honey Badger Behavior

Honey badgers are famous for being bold, clever, and stubborn.

How the honey badger gets food

  • Digging: It sniffs out prey hiding underground and digs it out fast with its strong claws.
  • Raiding nests: It breaks open beehives, bird nests, and termite mounds. Its thick skin protects it from most stings.
  • Hunting snakes: It will attack and eat snakes, even venomous ones.
  • Hunting buddies: Birds such as the pale chanting goshawk and animals such as jackals sometimes follow honey badgers. They catch the small animals that run away from the digging badger.

There is a famous story that a bird called the honeyguide leads honey badgers to beehives. It is a good story, but scientists have not been able to prove it happens.

How it protects itself from predators

Lions, leopards, and hyenas sometimes attack honey badgers, but they often give up. The honey badger defends itself by:

  • Puffing up, rattling, and growling loudly to look scary
  • Releasing a terrible smell from its stink pouch
  • Twisting inside its loose skin to bite an attacker that grabs it
  • Fighting back hard, even against much bigger animals

Sleeping habits

Honey badgers sleep in burrows, rock cracks, or hollow logs. In cool weather or quiet areas, they are often active during the day. In hot weather or near people, they become mostly nocturnal (active at night) and sleep through the day.

They live mostly alone and often sleep in a different burrow each day as they move around their home range.

Other interesting facts

Honey badgers are very clever. In zoos and wildlife centres, some have learned to open gates, stack objects to climb walls, and use sticks to reach things, which makes them famous escape artists.
