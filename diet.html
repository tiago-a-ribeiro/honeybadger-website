What Honey Badgers Eat

Despite the name, honey is only a small part of the menu. Honey badgers will eat almost anything they can catch or dig up.

What they eat

  • Small animals: mice, rats, gerbils, and other rodents – often the biggest part of the diet
  • Reptiles: lizards, small tortoises, and snakes, including venomous ones such as puff adders and cobras
  • Insects and other bugs: beetle larvae, termites, and scorpions
  • Bee nests: mostly the bee larvae (brood), plus honey
  • Birds and eggs, frogs, and sometimes carrion (dead animals)
  • Plants: berries, fruit, roots, and bulbs

How often and how much

How oftenEvery day. A honey badger forages for several hours in a row and may dig dozens of holes in one outing.
Meal sizeMostly many small meals, one small animal at a time. A big catch, like a large snake, can be enough food for a whole day.
How farTo find enough food, a honey badger may travel more than 20 km in a single day.

Honey badgers can survive bites from some venomous snakes that would kill other animals their size. Scientists think their bodies have some resistance to snake venom.
