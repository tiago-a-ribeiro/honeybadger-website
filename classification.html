Scientific Name and Classification

The honey badger is also called the ratel. Its scientific name is Mellivora capensis.

Primary species

Mellivora capensis is the only living species in its genus. The name means “honey-eater from the Cape”. Mellivora is Latin for “honey-eater”, and capensis refers to the Cape of Good Hope in South Africa, where it was first described in 1777.

Scientific classification

SpeciesMellivora capensis

Despite the name, the honey badger is not very closely related to other badgers. It is in its own subfamily, and its closest relatives are debated by scientists.

Sub-species

Scientists have described up to 12 sub-species, based mostly on where they live and small differences in size and colour. Experts do not all agree that every one is truly different.

  • M. c. capensis – southern Africa
  • M. c. abyssinica – Ethiopia
  • M. c. buechneri – Turkmenistan
  • M. c. concisa
  • M. c. cottoni
  • M. c. indica – India
  • M. c. inaurita – Nepal and nearby areas
  • M. c. leuconota – a form with an extra-white back
  • M. c. maxwelli
  • M. c. pumilio – southern Arabia
  • M. c. signata
  • M. c. wilsoni – Iran and Iraq
