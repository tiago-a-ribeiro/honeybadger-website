Anatomy and Biology

Small, stocky, and built like a tank: the honey badger's body is made for digging and for defending itself.

Size

Body lengthAbout 55–77 cm (22–30 in), not counting the tail
Tail lengthAbout 12–30 cm (5–12 in)
Height at shoulderAbout 23–28 cm (9–11 in)
WeightMales: about 9–16 kg (20–35 lb). Females: about 5–10 kg (11–22 lb). Males are much bigger than females.

Lifespan

Scientists do not know exactly how long honey badgers live in the wild. In zoos, they have lived to about 24 years. Wild badgers probably live much shorter lives because of predators, injuries, and hunger.

Unique physical features

  • Thick, loose skin: The skin is tough and very loose, especially around the neck. If a predator grabs it, the badger can twist around inside its skin and bite back.
  • Huge front claws: The front claws can be about 4 cm long. They are perfect for digging burrows and tearing open nests.
  • Strong jaws and teeth: They can crunch through bones and tortoise shells.
  • Tiny ears: The ears are just small ridges of skin. This helps keep dirt and bee stings out while digging.
  • Stink pouch: Like a skunk, a honey badger can release a strong-smelling liquid from a pouch under its tail.
  • Warning colours: The black body and pale grey back warn other animals to stay away.

How the honey badger breathes

Like all mammals, the honey badger breathes air with its lungs. A strong muscle called the diaphragm pulls air in through the nose and mouth. Oxygen moves from the lungs into the blood, and the heart pumps it around the body. Its deep, wide chest gives it plenty of lung power for long hours of digging and walking.
