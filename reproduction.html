Mating and Reproduction

Honey badger mothers raise their young alone, and they do it with a lot of care.

Mating

Honey badgers can breed at any time of the year. Males travel widely to find females and may mate with several females. After mating, the male leaves. He does not help raise the young.

Key facts

Gestation (pregnancy) periodAbout 50–70 days
Offspring at a timeUsually 1 cub, sometimes 2
Time with the motherAbout 12–16 months

Growing up

The cub is born blind and almost hairless in a burrow lined with grass. The mother moves her cub to a new burrow every few days, carrying it in her mouth, to keep it safe from predators.

When the cub is older, it follows its mother as she forages. It learns where to dig, what to eat, and how to deal with dangerous prey like snakes. By the time it leaves, the young badger is almost as big as its mother.

Because each cub stays with its mother for over a year, a female usually has only one litter every year and a half or so.
